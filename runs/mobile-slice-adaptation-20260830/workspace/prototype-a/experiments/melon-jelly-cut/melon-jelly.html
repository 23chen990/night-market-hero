Inside the experiment

Body. The slice is — tetrahedra over — particles, solved with small-step XPBD Neo-Hookean elasticity at a fixed 1/60 s step split into — substeps. Rind regions use roughly three times the flesh shear modulus. Hydrostatic elasticity resists volume change, and a positive-volume safeguard rejects inverted steps.

Knife. Pick up the knife (K), then draw across the slice. A view-aligned plane splits the tetrahedra and creates closed, deformable cut faces. Put down the knife to grab the independent pieces. Further cuts are supported within a 14,000-tet budget; grazing or unsafe cuts are rejected without removing material. Reset restores the uncut slice.

Motion. Damping removes only motion relative to the slice’s own rigid movement, so it still falls, slides and tumbles naturally before settling. The floor adds Coulomb friction and a soft, elastic bounce.

Surface. A —-vertex skin, — seeds and a few air bubbles are embedded in the tets with barycentric weights, so they stretch and rotate with the flesh.

Light. Native WebGPU and WGSL. Back faces are drawn first to measure how much jelly each view ray crosses; the front pass refracts a pre-rendered studio (floor, seeds, bubbles), tints it by Beer–Lambert absorption, adds scattering and thin-edge transmission, Fresnel reflections of procedural softboxes and GGX highlights. The contact shadow comes from a top-down height map of the deformed slice.
